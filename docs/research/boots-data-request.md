# Proposed request for annotated speech-to-beatbox audio

Status: local draft only. Nothing has been sent. Sending requires explicit user authorization because it contacts another person.

Recipient: Nathalie Henrich Bernardoni <Nathalie.Henrich@gipsa-lab.fr>

The address is published on her [institutional homepage](https://www.gipsa-lab.grenoble-inp.fr/~nathalie.henrich/index.html). The requested material is described in [Paroni et al., JEP2020, section2](https://aclanthology.org/2020.jeptalnrecital-jep.53.pdf); the public JASA supplement does not include these phrase recordings or their TextGrid files.

Subject: Access to annotated “boots and cats” recordings for a beatbox-to-MIDI project

Dear Dr. Henrich Bernardoni,

I’m building BEATBOX, a personal open-source project that converts monophonic vocal percussion into MIDI while preserving the performer’s original timing: https://github.com/g-h-miles/beatbox.

Your 2020 paper with Annalisa Paroni, “Beatboxer, est-ce parler ?”, describes spoken and beatboxed “boots and cats” repetitions and manually marked burst timestamps in Praat TextGrid files. Would it be possible to access the corresponding audio and annotations for evaluating the project? The public Zenodo supplement contains useful individual sound examples, but I could not find those phrase recordings there.

I would also appreciate any guidance on available annotated recordings from multiple performers, particularly different snare techniques. Could you clarify the permitted uses, attribution requirements, and whether model training or public release of trained weights would be allowed? Evaluation-only access would also be useful. I would not redistribute recordings or annotations without permission.

The project currently falls short of reliable recognition across unfamiliar voices. I’m seeking independently labeled recordings to measure that honestly, including the transition from ordinary speech to beatboxing.

Thank you for considering the request,
Graham

## Why this request is relevant

The requested phrase recordings would address a missing evaluation condition, not by themselves establish >95% generalization. The study describes one speaker, so a separate multi-speaker corpus remains necessary for broad claims. Requested access is not assumed granted, and any permission would need to be checked against the actual permitted use before training or publication. This is development data acquisition; it does not require app users to train a model.
